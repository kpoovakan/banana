### a summary of info found on https://www.energy.gov/ne/articles/5-radioactive-products-we-use-every-day

Radioisotopes are unstable atoms that decay and emit excess energy. That emission of excess energy is known as radiation. Radioisotopes can be naturally occuring or artificial isotopes of an element.

Bananas contain potassium-40, which is a radionuclide that lets it emit .01 millirem (0.1 microsieverts). Eating a banana presents more radiation exposure than if the hooman were to stand next to a spent nuclear fuel dry cask or a nuclear power plant.

### a summary of info found on https://www.britannica.com/science/Are-Bananas-Radioactive

Most potassium atoms are a stable form known as potassium-39, containing 19 protons and 20 neutrons. A tiny fraction (about 0.01 percent) of potassium atoms are a radioactive isotope known as potassium-40, which has one extra neutron. That additional neutron makes the nucleus unstable, so it occasionally decays into another element and emits radiation in the process.

### explanation of isotopes from https://www.unitsconverters.com/en/Sievert-To-Microsievert/Unittounit-4006-4008
A family of people often consists of related but not identical individuals. Elements have families as well, known as isotopes. Isotopes are members of a family of an element that all have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

### the math
Lethal Dose (LD): "The dose of radiation expected to cause death to 50 percent of an exposed population within 30 days ... the LD is in the range from ... 4 to 5 sieverts received over a very short period." https://www.nrc.gov/education-regulatory-research/glossary/lethal-dose-ld 
One sievert is 1,000,000 microsieverts (1 million) according to https://www.unitsconverters.com/en/Sievert-To-Microsievert/Unittounit-4006-4008 
So, 5 sieverts is 5 million microsieverts. 0.1 microsieverts per banana, so 5 million divided by 0.1 is 50 million bananas. All at once. Do you like bananas enough to eat 50 million bananas all at once?
